Before You Dial
Think this

You are a doctor making rounds

You are not selling. You are diagnosing. Most businesses in India are bleeding money through a broken digital presence — you are calling to find out if they're one of them. That is a service, not a pitch.

Rejection reframe

"No" is data, not failure

Hormozi's rule: volume cures all. A "no" removes an unqualified prospect. It brings you closer to the "yes." Never argue. Never beg. Thank them and dial the next number.

Track these

Metrics that matter

Dials per day. Pickups per 100 dials. Conversations per 100 pickups. Meetings booked per 100 conversations. Nothing else matters on day one.

Ignore these

What NOT to focus on

Your accent. Your lack of case studies. The prospect's tone in the first 5 seconds. Whether they sound busy. These are distractions. Focus on the next word out of your mouth.

Hormozi's core belief

No case studies? Fine. Lead with a free audit. Hormozi calls this the "diagnosis before prescription" model. You are not asking them to trust your past work. You are asking them to let you show them a problem they already have — for free. That is an almost frictionless yes.

Opening Hooks
Opener 1 · Best performing

The Problem-First Opener

Smile "Hi, am I speaking with [Name / Owner]?"
Pause
Confident "I'm Arjun calling from Chennai. Quick question — when someone Googles your business right now, are they actually finding you?"

Why it works: Opens with a question about their reality, not your product. Forces them to think about a gap they may have never articulated. The word "actually" carries implicit doubt — a pattern interrupt.

Opener 2 · Alternative

The Observation Opener

Smile "Hi, is this [Business Name]?"
Pause
"I was looking at your business online and I noticed a few things that could be costing you customers. I'm not selling anything right now — I just wanted to ask you one question."

Why it works: "I was looking at your business" signals research and specificity. "Not selling anything" is a permission reset — it lowers their guard before you ask.

Opener 3 · Permission-based

The 30-Second Ask

Smile "Hi, this is Arjun. I know you didn't expect my call. Can I take 30 seconds to tell you why I'm calling, and if it's not relevant you can just tell me and I'll hang up?"

Why it works: Gives them control. The brain hates losing control — giving it back immediately disarms hostility. Works brilliantly with assertive Indian business owners who feel their time is being taken.

Opener 4 · Pattern interrupt

The Blunt Opener (Use sparingly)

Lower voice "Hi, honest question — is your website actually bringing you any customers right now, or is it just sitting there?"

Why it works: "Honest question" triggers a micro-commitment to honesty. "Just sitting there" is conversational and often hits a nerve — because for most SMEs, that's the truth. Use after warming up with Opener 1 for a few weeks.

Discovery Questions

Don't fire these as a list. Pick 2–3 based on what they say. Listen more than you talk. Let silence do work.

1

"How long have you had your current website — and when did you last update it?"

2

"When a potential customer searches for someone like you in [City], do you know if you're appearing on the first page of Google?"
Intent: creates awareness gap
3

"Right now, where does most of your business come from — referrals, walk-ins, calls, or online?"
Intent: reveals dependency on offline only

"Has it ever happened that a customer told you they tried to find your contact online and had trouble?"
Intent: makes invisible pain visible
5
Revenue Aspiration
"If you could get even 5–10 more qualified customers per month just from online — what would that mean for your business?"
Intent: anchors the value of your solution
6
Competition Frame
"Are you seeing more of your competitors showing up online? Has that affected anything for you?"
Intent: triggers loss aversion — most powerful motivator
7
No Website — Direct
"If someone wanted to check your business out before calling you — where would they go right now?"
Intent: makes the absence embarrassingly obvious

Never sell a website. Sell what the website does. A website is a box. The outcome is customers finding them, trusting them, and calling them at 11pm without them lifting a finger.

Closing The Call

Remember: the only goal is a booked meeting. Don't close on price. Don't close on services. Close on time.

Soft close
"Would it be okay if we blocked just 20 minutes sometime this week? I'll come prepared with some data on your current online presence."
Direct close
"Are you free Wednesday or Thursday — morning or afternoon?"
Pause Give two options. Never ask open-ended.
Calendar booking close
"Perfect. If you give me your WhatsApp number I'll send a calendar invite with the meeting link. Takes 30 seconds and you'll have it in writing."
WhatsApp follow-up close
"No worries. Let me send you something on WhatsApp so you have our details — and I'll also include a quick snapshot of what your site's current Google ranking looks like. Fair?"

Coaching Notes

Beginner mistake #1

Talking too much. Hormozi says: the person asking questions is in control. Pitch after you discover — never before.

Beginner mistake #2

Getting emotional after rejection. 97% of calls end in no. That is normal. That is the business. Act like a doctor — detached, professional, curious.

Beginner mistake #3

Leading with "we are a web design company." Nobody cares what you do. They care what you can do FOR THEM. Lead with their problem.

Beginner mistake #4

Reading the script robotically. Use it as guardrails, not a prison. Know it so well that it sounds like a real conversation.

Daily call target

Aim for 60–80 dials per day. 30–40 minimum. At 60 dials/day for 5 days: 300 calls → 3–5 meetings. That's a pipeline.

After every 100 calls

Review: What was the most common objection? Which opener had the highest pickup rate? What sentence ended most conversations? Adjust one thing. Repeat.

India-specific tip

WhatsApp is your CRM. Send a voice note follow-up within 2 hours of a positive call. It stands out completely. Almost no one does it.

What Hormozi would critique

Weak calls make excuses for the prospect. "They seemed busy." "Their business is small." Hormozi's response: irrelevant. Dial the next number. Volume is the answer.

Final word

"The only way to get good at sales is to do sales. The script is a starting point. Your first 100 calls will be awkward. Your next 100 will be better. By call 500, you'll be dangerous. Most people quit at call 20. Don't be most people."
